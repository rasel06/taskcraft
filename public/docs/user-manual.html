Overview

Welcome to TaskCraft

TaskCraft is where your team organizes work: teams own projects, projects contain issues, and issues move across a small set of statuses from idea to done. This manual covers every screen a member or admin will use, in the order you're likely to meet them.

Each section stands on its own — use the contents on the left (or the strip at the top, on a phone) to jump straight to what you need. Procedures are numbered; anything worth a second glance is called out in a colored box like the one below.

Note

Boxes like this one flag things worth knowing. Tip boxes suggest a faster way to work, and Caution boxes mark actions you can't undo.

Getting started

Signing in

TaskCraft accounts are created by a workspace admin — there is no public sign-up page. If you don't have credentials yet, ask your admin to invite you (see Members).

  1. Go to the login page.

    You'll land here automatically if you're not signed in.

  2. Enter your email and password.

    Both fields are required.

  3. Select "Sign in."

    You'll be taken straight to your Inbox.

Staying signed in, and signing out

Once you're in, your session lasts 30 days on that browser. To sign out, open the account menu at the bottom of the left sidebar (your avatar and name) and choose Log out.

Note

There's no self-service "forgot password" link. If you're locked out, a workspace admin can reset your password from Settings → Members — this immediately signs you out everywhere, so you'll need the new password next time.

Worked example

Quick start: your first tracked issue in five steps

A concrete walkthrough tying the core concepts together. Each step links to the full section if you want more detail.

  1. Sign in.

    Use alice@taskcraft.dev / password123 on a demo workspace, or your own credentials.

  2. Create a team.

    Click the + next to "Teams" in the sidebar, name it Marketing — TaskCraft proposes the identifier MAR for you — and select Create team. See Teams.

  3. Create a project.

    Select + Project at the top of the sidebar, name it Q4 Launch Campaign, set Team to Marketing, and select Publish. See Projects.

  4. Create an issue.

    Select + Issue, title it Draft launch announcement, set Project to Q4 Launch Campaign, Priority to High, and assign it to yourself. See Issues.

  5. Find it again.

    Open My Issues in the sidebar — your new issue is there, since you're the assignee. See Your views.

Doing the work

Issues

An issue is a single unit of work — a bug, a task, a piece of a project. Every issue belongs to exactly one project, and gets an ID built from its team's identifier and a running number, like FRO-42.

Good to know

There's no due date or sub-issues on an issue today — description, labels, and the discussion thread below are the available detail fields. The Attachments field on this create form just records file names for reference; the files themselves aren't uploaded. For real file uploads, use the discussion thread's attachment button once the issue exists (see Discussion below).

Viewing and editing an issue

Select any issue card or row to open it. The dialog opens on the Detail view: edit the description (saves automatically when you click away), and change Status, Priority, or Assignee directly from their dropdowns — each change saves immediately, with a confirmation toast. Two pill buttons at the bottom — Discussion and Activity — switch the dialog to those views; a back arrow returns you to Detail.

Caution

If you have permission to delete issues, a trash icon appears in the issue view. Deleting is immediate and permanent — there's no confirmation step and no undo.

Discussion

Every issue has a discussion thread, visible to anyone who can see the issue. Messages appear as chat bubbles — yours on the right, everyone else's on the left — so it's obvious at a glance who said what.

  • Open it two ways. Select the speech-bubble icon directly on an issue's card or row to jump straight into Discussion, or open the issue and select the Discussion pill from Detail.
  • Replies are one level deep. Selecting "Reply" on any message — including another reply — always attaches your response to that message's top-level parent, keeping the thread flat and easy to scan.
  • Edit or delete your own messages only. Every message shows "Reply," and your own also show "Edit" and "Delete" — there's no moderator override, so once you delete a message it's gone for good (the fact that a comment existed still shows up in the Activity log).
  • Attach files. The paperclip button on the composer (and on each reply box) uploads images (PNG/JPEG/GIF/WEBP/SVG), PDFs, Word docs, or text/CSV/Markdown files, up to 10 MB each. Images preview inline; other files show as a named, downloadable card. Unlike the Attachments field on the create-issue form, these are real uploaded files.
  • New messages arrive instantly. Your own comment or reply appears the moment you send it, before the server confirms it — no waiting on a page refresh.

A small dot on an issue's discussion icon means there's a comment you haven't seen yet; it clears once you open the Discussion view. The Activity view is a read-only timeline of every field change, comment, reply, edit, and deletion on the issue.

Boards: filtering, grouping, and sorting

Anywhere you see a list of issues — My Issues, Views, a team's Issues page, a project, a cycle — you get the same toolbar:

  • Search — filters the visible list by title or ID as you type.
  • + Filter — narrow the list to one or more priorities.
  • Group — organize into columns/sections by Status, Priority, or Assignee.
  • Order — sort by Created date, Priority, or Title.
  • Board / List — switch between kanban columns and a flat row list.

Tip

In Board view grouped by Status, drag a card into a different column to change its status in one motion — for example, dragging FRO-12 from Todo into In Progress updates it instantly, the same as changing the Status field by hand.

Draft vs. published

At the bottom of the drawer, choose Save as draft to keep a project visible only to you and the members you added, or Publish to make it visible to everyone with access to its team. Draft projects show a "Draft" badge everywhere they appear, and only published projects show up on Roadmaps.

The project page

Opening a project shows its status and priority (editable inline at the top), its lead and timeline, the full issue board scoped to that project, and — on wider screens — a sidebar with the project brief and its milestones, which you can add to, edit, or check off at any time.

Project roles and members

Alongside its Members list (added when creating the project, or later), a project has its own Settings page — select the gear icon at the top of the project page. Anyone with the manage_teams permission, or who's already a member of the project (any role), can open it to add or remove members and set each member's project role to Admin or Member.

Note

A project's Admin/Member role is scoped to that one project, separate from both the member's workspace role and any team-level Admin badge (see Teams) — the same person can be a plain Member on one project and an Admin on another.

Teams

A team is the organizational home for a group of projects — most workspaces have a handful, one per function or product area.

Creating a team

  1. Select the + next to "Teams" in the sidebar.
  2. Name the team.

    TaskCraft proposes a short Identifier from the initials as you type — "Frontend" becomes FRO — which you can edit directly.

  3. Pick an icon and color, a Team lead, and a timezone.

    The Team lead defaults to you.

  4. Choose Private or Public.

    Public teams are visible to the whole workspace; private teams are visible only to their members and anyone with the view_all_teams permission.

  5. Select "Create team."

Optionally, use Clone settings from to copy an existing team's icon, color, and timezone as a starting point.

Team lead

Every team has exactly one Team lead — the person accountable for it, shown wherever teams are listed. Set it when you create the team, or change it later from the team's Settings page. The lead is always a member of the team, and TaskCraft won't let you remove them from the team while they still hold the role — reassign the lead first.

Team settings and members

Open a team, then its Settings tab. Anyone with the manage_teams permission can rename the team, change its lead, timezone, or privacy, and add or remove members from the Members list below. The Identifier can't be changed once a team is created, since it's baked into every issue ID.

Note

The shield icon next to a member's name in Team Settings marks them as that team's local admin — a separate thing from their workspace-wide role. See Roles & permissions for the distinction.

Cycles

A cycle is a time-boxed sprint scoped to one team — a way to say "this is what we're working on this fortnight." Open a team, then its Cycles tab.

A cycle's status is calculated from its dates, not set by hand:

Upcoming › Active › Completed

Select New cycle, give it an optional name (otherwise it's just "Cycle 5," numbered automatically), and set a start and target date. Open any cycle to add issues to it from a dropdown of the team's issues, track a progress bar of how many are Done or Cancelled, and work its board the same way as any other issue list.

Roadmaps

Roadmaps show published projects on a horizontal timeline, sized and positioned by their start and target dates — a bird's-eye view of what's shipping when.

  • Roadmaps (sidebar) — every published project you can see, across every team.
  • A team's Roadmaps tab — the same view, scoped to that one team.

Use the search box and the Status/Team filters to narrow a busy roadmap, and select any row to jump to that project. Projects without a start or target date show "No timeline set" instead of a bar.

Doing the work

Inbox, My Issues & Views

Three sidebar links look similar but answer different questions. Here's the difference at a glance:

Inbox

Issues assigned to you, as a simple list, most recently updated first. Your fastest daily check-in.

My Issues

The same issues assigned to you, but on the full board — with search, filters, grouping, and the board/list toggle.

Views

Every issue you can see across every team you have access to — the whole workspace on one board, labeled "All Issues" on the page itself.

Settings

Personal settings

Open Settings from the account menu at the bottom of the sidebar. The Personal group covers your own account and preferences.

PageWhat you can do
ProfileChange your display name. Your email is fixed, and your avatar is generated from your initials.
NotificationsToggle alerts for assignments, mentions, status changes, and a weekly digest.

Good to know

Notifications and Preferences are saved to this browser only, not to your account — they won't follow you to a different device, and other people can't see them.

Reference lists (Issues & Projects settings)

Under Settings, the Issues and Projects groups hold personal reference lists: suggested labels, named templates, and response-time targets for issues; suggested labels and templates for projects. Add, rename, or remove entries freely — they're a private checklist for you to work from when filling in an issue or project by hand.

Good to know

These lists are saved to your browser and are for your own reference — they aren't enforced when you actually create an issue or project (any label or text still works), and they're not shared with teammates. Statuses and Updates, further down the Projects group, are read-only summaries instead: the fixed project lifecycle, and a feed of every published project's current status.

Workspace settings

The Workspace group is where admins manage the people, teams, and structure of the whole instance. Most of these pages need a specific permission — see Roles & permissions.

Members

Lists everyone in the workspace with their role, email, and TaskCraft's identity fields (Bank ID, File number, Mobile). From here you can:

  • Invite people — set their name, email, identity fields, an initial password, and their workspace role.
  • Edit a member's details or role.
  • Reset password — sets a new password for them and signs them out everywhere.
  • Delete a member — their past issues stay in place, just unassigned.

Note

Inviting someone sets their password directly — there's no emailed invite link. Share the initial password with them through a separate, secure channel.

Teams, Billing, Integrations

The Teams page lists every team for quick access to its settings. Billing and Integrations are placeholders in this self-hosted build — Billing shows the current plan with no payment flow, and Integrations offers a GitHub connection that isn't wired up to a real GitHub app yet.

Features

A set of workspace-wide feature switches (AI & Agents, Initiatives, Documents, Customer requests, Releases) for previewing what a fuller TaskCraft could include. Today they're bookmarks for the roadmap rather than live functionality.

Roles & permissions

Every member has exactly one workspace role, and every role grants some subset of five permissions. There's no partial credit or inheritance — a permission is either on or off for that role.

Creating a custom role

Requires the Manage roles permission. Go to Settings → Roles → New role, name it, and toggle on whichever of the five permissions it should carry — for example, a "Team Lead" role with only Manage teams switched on. Built-in roles (Admin, Member) can be renamed or re-permissioned but not deleted; custom roles can be edited or deleted freely.

Assigning a role

Go to Settings → Members, select Edit on the member, and choose their role from the dropdown. The change applies the moment you save.

Note

Don't confuse this workspace role with a team's local Admin badge (see Teams) — the badge only affects an icon shown in that team's member list, while the workspace role is what actually gates pages and buttons across TaskCraft.

Frequently asked questions

Can I recover a deleted issue?

No. Deleting an issue is immediate and permanent, with no confirmation step — double-check before you select the trash icon.

Why don't my Notification or Preference settings show up on another computer?

Those pages save to the browser you're using, not to your account, so they're local to each device.

Who can see a private team?

Its own members, plus anyone whose workspace role has the "View all teams" permission — everyone else won't see it in search, the sidebar, or roadmaps.

What happens to a member's issues if they're removed from the workspace?

The issues stay exactly where they are; they simply become unassigned.

Can I remove the Team lead from a team?

Not directly — TaskCraft blocks removing a team's current lead as a member. Assign a new lead in Team Settings first, then remove the old one if needed.
